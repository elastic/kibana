How to care for your trail shoes

Good boots last for years when they are cleaned and dried properly. Remove the insoles after every long hike and let both parts air out away from direct heat.

Never dry leather next to a radiator; it cracks the fibres.

Brush off dry mud before it is washed. A soft brush and lukewarm water are enough for most uppers, and a small amount of mild soap handles stubborn stains.

Re-proof the shoes when water no longer beads on the surface. Apply the treatment while the uppers are still slightly damp, then leave them to dry overnight.

Check the laces and eyelets regularly. Replacing a worn lace costs little and prevents a blown-out eyelet on the trail.

Store shoes loosely laced in a cool, ventilated place. Stuffing them with newspaper helps them keep their shape and absorbs leftover moisture.
