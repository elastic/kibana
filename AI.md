# AI-assisted contributions

We're glad you're here, and yes, we welcome contributions made with the help of AI. These tools have become a normal part of how many of us write software, and used well they let you move faster and spend your energy on the parts of a problem that actually matter. The one thing we ask is simple: you need to understand the change you're proposing. AI works best as a force multiplier for work you already grasp, not as a way to produce code you can't read, reason about, or defend.

A good place to start is a conversation. Before you write anything, open an issue or leave a comment on one that already exists and let us know what you're thinking. It gives the team a chance to point you toward the right approach, flag anything you might not be aware of, and save you from building something that won't fit. A few minutes of back-and-forth up front tends to be worth far more than a large surprise pull request later.

Keep your changes tight and easy to reason about. The contributions that land most smoothly are the ones with clear edges: a well-understood bug fix, a documentation improvement, a missing test, an accessibility enhancement. Work you can run, poke at, and verify end to end yourself is work we can review with confidence. Sprawling changes that touch a bit of everything are hard for anyone to evaluate, no matter how they were written.

When you open a pull request, let it show that you understand what you're submitting. Read your own diff. Run the code. Be ready to explain why each part is there, because that's exactly what a reviewer will ask. And when they do ask, answer them yourself. We're happy to use AI to write code, but the discussion around a change should be a real exchange between people. If a maintainer poses a question, they're looking for your thinking, not a generated reply.

The strongest AI-assisted work we've seen all shared the same shape. Someone understood the problem, talked it through with the team, scoped it carefully, and used AI to get there faster. Every line of the diff was something they could stand behind. Aim for that, and you'll fit right in.
