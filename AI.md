# AI-Assisted Code Contributions

We're glad you're here, and we welcome contributions made with the help of AI tools. Coding assistants can help you understand a large codebase faster, spot patterns, and iterate on ideas. Used well, they make good contributors even better. This page explains how to keep that help pointed in the right direction.

## Start by talking to us

Before you write any code, tell us what you have in mind. Open an issue, comment on one that already exists, or start a discussion. A quick conversation up front lets maintainers point you toward the right approach, flag work that's already underway, and save you from investing effort in something that won't land. We'd much rather help you shape an idea early than turn away a finished PR that went the wrong direction.

## Keep your change focused

The contributions that go smoothly are the ones you can stand behind from start to finish. A clear bug fix, a documentation improvement, a missing test, or an accessibility enhancement all share the same quality: the change is small enough that you can verify it works end to end. When a PR does one thing well, it's easier for you to reason about and easier for us to review.

## Know what your change does

Whatever tools you use to get there, the code you submit is yours. Read your own diff, run it, and make sure you can explain why every part of it is the way it is. If a reviewer asks about a decision, you should have an answer that comes from your own understanding. AI is a great way to move faster on work you understand. It's a poor way to produce work you can't evaluate, and that gap tends to show.

## Speak for yourself

The same goes for the conversation around your contribution. When you reply to a review comment or discuss an issue, share your own thinking in your own words. Pasting generated text into a thread rarely helps, and maintainers can usually tell. Genuine back-and-forth is how trust gets built, and it's how we all end up with a better result.

## The bottom line

AI is a force multiplier, not a stand-in for the contributor. We're happy to see people use it to do good work more quickly. What doesn't work for us is a wave of auto-generated changes from someone who hasn't run them, can't explain them, or isn't really engaged. Stay in the driver's seat, and your AI-assisted contributions are very welcome.
