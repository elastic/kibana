# Using AI to contribute to Kibana

AI tools have made it easier than ever to write code, and we're genuinely glad when people use them to contribute to Kibana. Some of the best contributions we see come from someone who understood a problem well, aligned with the team on how to solve it, and then leaned on AI to move quickly. This page is about how to make that kind of contribution land well.

## Start with a conversation

Before you write any code, talk to us. Open an issue describing what you want to change, or comment on an existing one to let us know you're picking it up. A short exchange up front helps us point you toward the right approach, flag anything you might not be aware of, and save you from building something that won't fit. It also means that by the time your pull request shows up, we already know the story behind it and can review it faster.

## Keep it focused

The contributions that go smoothly are almost always small and self-contained. A bug fix, a documentation improvement, a missing test, an accessibility fix. Pick something you can hold in your head and verify end to end on your own machine. A tightly scoped change is easier for you to reason about and easier for us to review, and that combination is what gets things merged.

## Know your own change

Read your diff before you send it. Run the code. Make sure you can explain, in your own words, why every part of the change is there and why it's correct. When a pull request clearly reflects that kind of understanding, it stands out immediately and moves through review quickly. And if there's a line you can't account for, that's a sign the change isn't ready yet. Take the time to understand it first.

## Let AI assist your judgment, not replace it

AI is fantastic for accelerating work you already understand. It's not a substitute for understanding the codebase, the problem, or the shape of a good solution. That distinction matters most in the parts that aren't code. When you discuss a change with a maintainer, we want to hear from you, in your own voice, working through the reasoning together. Pasted-in generated replies tend to stall a conversation rather than move it forward.

## The pattern that works

Understand the problem. Align with the team. Use AI to help you build. Then review and validate the result carefully yourself. When those pieces are in place, AI becomes a real force multiplier, and we're happy to have the help.

The flip side is worth being honest about. A large, generated pull request that its author can't explain costs the whole team time we'd rather spend helping thoughtful contributors. So bring your understanding along with your code, and you'll find Kibana a welcoming place to contribute.
